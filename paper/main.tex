\begin{abstract}
Answering complex questions over semi-structured tables and text requires preserving structural tabular context while connecting multi-hop evidence from external passages. Existing retrieval-augmented generation approaches face a fundamental dilemma: flattening tables into plain text obscures hierarchical header associations and intra-row bindings, whereas graph-based methods typically prune evidence into a few linear paths, inadvertently discarding vital peripheral facts and failing under structural candidate ties. We propose the Online Unified Evidence Graph framework for retrieval-augmented generation, constructing a bounded, schema-preserving multigraph dynamically at query time. The proposed framework combines a two-stage retriever with hyperlink-guided bridge passage expansion, preserving qualified headers and row bindings alongside deterministic text triples. Crucially, rather than applying destructive path filtering, the architecture couples complete evidence subgraph serialization with online grounded path traversal---leveraging dynamically enumerated multi-hop paths as non-filtering reading guides to steer language model attention without losing contextual evidence. Furthermore, the framework introduces an active structural ambiguity evaluator that detects candidate score collisions and supplies lexical tiebreak signals. Comprehensive evaluations on the HybridQA benchmark demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly improves grounding accuracy, Exact Match, and Semantic Exact Match over direct prompting and path-filtering baselines while delivering an auditable, code-free reasoning trace.
\keywords{Knowledge graphs \and Retrieval-augmented generation \and Table question answering \and Grounded reasoning}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Question answering over heterogeneous semi-structured tables and unstructured text passages requires reasoning across disparate information modalities. In the standard HybridQA benchmark~\cite{hybridqa}, answering a multi-hop question requires locating an entity within a table cell, traversing its Wikipedia hyperlink to an external unstructured passage (or vice-versa), and extracting the grounded answer span. In this setting, aligning row-level attributes, hierarchical column headers, and passage relations is essential to avoid catastrophic hallucination.

The predominant baseline in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm,gao2023ragsurvey} is Direct LLM prompting (in-context generation), where serialized table rows and retrieved passages are supplied directly to the language model. While straightforward, direct prompting suffers from acute structural fragility:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Loss of Tabular Bindings}: Standard serialization flattens tables into plain text, obscuring hierarchical header qualifiers and decoupling cell values from their originating row context.
    \item \emph{Structural Score Collisions and Ambiguity}: In complex table queries, multiple candidate entities across different rows often satisfy coarse relational constraints equally well. Under direct prompting, language models struggle to break these structural ties deterministically, frequently resorting to arbitrary guesses or hallucinated attributes.
\end{enumerate}

To impart structure, previous graph-augmented methods construct knowledge graphs and extract candidate reasoning paths~\cite{yasunaga2021qagnn,pan2024unifying,peng2024graphragsurvey}. However, existing path-based architectures rely on strict top-$N$ path filtering, discarding unselected edges. This introduces \emph{evidence loss}: linear paths prune away peripheral attributes (e.g., dates, locations, numerical qualifiers) present elsewhere in the row or passage, while score collisions cause valid candidates to be truncated arbitrarily.

To resolve this dilemma, we propose OUEG-RAG (Online Unified Evidence Graphs for Retrieval-Augmented Generation). OUEG-RAG is organized around the principle of \emph{complete subgraph preservation with guided online path traversal}. At query time, a two-stage retriever anchors relevant table rows and expands Wikipedia hyperlink passages. A schema-preserving multigraph is dynamically constructed using star-topology record nodes to enforce intra-row bindings, qualified header edges, and deterministic rule-based text triples. Rather than applying destructive path pruning, OUEG-RAG serializes the complete evidence multigraph via \emph{lossless subgraph prompt compression} while enumerating grounded multi-hop paths as \emph{non-filtering suggested reading guides}. Furthermore, OUEG-RAG introduces an active \emph{structural ambiguity evaluator} that detects candidate score collisions and supplies lexical tiebreak signals to resolve ties deterministically.

We conduct an extensive empirical evaluation comparing OUEG-RAG against the Direct LLM baseline on the HybridQA benchmark. Rather than relying on rigid offline pipelines or heuristic prompt engineering, our empirical findings demonstrate three consistent advantages:
(1)~\emph{Generalizable Grounding Gains}: Structuring heterogeneous table cells and linked text into a schema-preserving multigraph consistently improves exact match and token-level F1 across different model scales, achieving substantial net positive margins in head-to-head pairwise evaluations.
(2)~\emph{Decisive Resilience Under Structural Ambiguity}: The empirical advantage of OUEG-RAG is particularly pronounced on complex queries exhibiting structural score collisions across candidate table rows. On this subset, active score collision detection and lexical tiebreaking systematically avert the arbitrary guesses and hallucinations that plague direct prompting.
(3)~\emph{Preservation of Context Integrity}: By bounding candidate table rows and external hyperlink passages within a compressed evidence multigraph, OUEG-RAG eliminates context-budget truncation errors while incurring a modest, predictable latency trade-off.
A comprehensive quantitative breakdown across metrics, model configurations, ambiguity partitions, and runtime latencies is presented in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} and Section~\ref{sec:results} (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}).


We articulate four main methodological contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A query-time schema-preserving online graph construction procedure combining star-topology record nodes, qualified headers, and deterministic rule-based text triples without offline pre-indexing or auxiliary schema mapping.
    \item An online graph-and-path unified architecture (OUEG-RAG) that reconciles full evidence subgraph preservation via prompt compression with online guided path reading without destructive filtering.
    \item \emph{Structural Ambiguity Assessment and Tiebreak (Ambiguity Assessment \& Tiebreak)}: An active mechanism that automatically detects structural score-collision ties when multiple candidate entities satisfy relational constraints with identical graph scores, resolving ambiguities via bridge lexical keywords to prevent hallucination.
    \item \emph{Empirical Verification Across Model Scales}: A comprehensive head-to-head evaluation across model scales, demonstrating consistent gains in Exact Match, Token F1, and resilience against context truncation over Direct LLM prompting on HybridQA.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\input{figures/paradigm-comparison}

\paragraph{Hybrid Table--Text Question Answering.}
Multi-hop reasoning over heterogeneous tabular and textual data was catalyzed by HybridQA~\cite{hybridqa}, which introduced the seminal HYBRIDER baseline combining coarse row retrieval with a fine-tuned cross-modal machine reading comprehension reader. Subsequent specialized neural architectures advanced table--text comprehension through multi-view attention mechanisms over cell and text tokens (MATE~\cite{eisenschlos2021mate}), multi-hop document-hopping retrieval (DocHopper~\cite{sun2021dochopper}), and multi-instance cell-passage linking (MITQA~\cite{kumar2021mitqa}). To capture complex structural dependencies, graph-based HybridQA methods construct multi-granularity evidence graphs containing row, cell, and sentence nodes, such as MuGER²~\cite{chen2022muger2} and S3HQA~\cite{lei2023s3hqa}, training graph neural networks (GNNs) or sequential samplers via supervised fine-tuning. While effective, these early systems fundamentally rely on task-specific parameter training over thousands of labeled instances. In contrast, OUEG-RAG operates in the modern training-free in-context learning regime, empowering frozen open-weights LLMs to reason over heterogeneous tables and text zero-shot without fine-tuning or task-specific parameters.

\paragraph{Table Reasoning with Large Language Models.}
Recent research leverages LLMs for table reasoning through symbolic programs or decomposition. Programmatic frameworks bind LLMs to programming languages such as SQL or Python (Binder~\cite{cheng2023binder}, TabSQLify), iteratively decompose complex table queries into sub-questions (DATER~\cite{ye2023dater}), or progressively evolve tabular states through stepwise operations (Chain-of-Table~\cite{wang2024chainoftable}). Concurrently, TableRAG architectures~\cite{chen2024tablerag,yu2025tablerag} scale retrieval across large documents and tabular schemas. While symbolic execution excels on structured arithmetic over clean isolated tables, it falters in hybrid multi-hop settings where the critical reasoning step requires traversing unstructured Wikipedia passages linked to table cells. Code interpreters cannot natively evaluate fuzzy semantic hops across free text without fragile ad-hoc prompts or multi-turn sandbox loops. OUEG-RAG bridges this gap by unifying semi-structured table cells and external text passages into a single symbolic evidence multigraph evaluated in a single, code-free LLM call.

\paragraph{Contextualizing Star-Topology and Knowledge Graph Paradigms.}
Extracting structured representations from tables and texts encompasses three prior paradigms alongside our proposed framework (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigms}): Semantic Table Interpretation (STI)~\cite{ritze2016matching,deng2020turl} relies on rigid ontology-matching pipelines (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigms}a); LLM-driven open triple extraction~\cite{edge2024graphrag,pan2024unifying} hallucinates phantom edges and destroys tabular row bindings (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigms}b); and persistent GraphRAG~\cite{edge2024graphrag,tan2025hydrarag,peng2024graphragsurvey} requires static pre-indexing and aggressive top-$k$ pruning that discards peripheral cells (Fig.~\ref{fig:paradigms}c).

While earlier HybridQA graph systems (e.g., MuGER², MITQA, S3HQA) explored heterogeneous row and cell node abstractions for neural GNN message passing, the role of our star-topology ($\rho_i \rightarrow v_{i,j}$) is fundamentally different: it is an \emph{in-context symbolic serialization mechanism}. In LLM prompt generation, direct table flattening destroys horizontal row bindings, whereas naive triple extraction causes entity disconnection or token explosion. OUEG-RAG's record hubs $\rho_i$ explicitly bind multi-column cell attributes horizontally within a compressed, lossless prompt representation. Furthermore, OUEG-RAG departs from existing graph paradigms across three key principles: (1)~\emph{Lossless Subgraph vs.\ Destructive Pruning}: rather than discarding peripheral cells via top-$k$ path pruning, it preserves the complete evidence multigraph losslessly while employing candidate paths strictly as non-filtering reading guides; (2)~\emph{Structural Collision Tiebreaking}: when candidate rows share identical graph scores, OUEG-RAG resolves collisions deterministically ($\tau(c)$) via bridge lexical keywords rather than arbitrary truncation; and (3)~\emph{Zero-LLM Graph Construction}: graph construction uses deterministic heuristic rules executed in $<20$\,ms, enabling single-turn greedy generation ($T=0$).

\paragraph{Cross-Source Graph-Augmented Reasoning.}
Augmenting language models with structured knowledge graphs captures non-local multi-hop dependencies that keyword and dense retrieval miss, as demonstrated by QA-GNN~\cite{yasunaga2021qagnn}, JointLK~\cite{sun2022jointlk}, KAPING~\cite{baek2023kaped}, and G-Retriever~\cite{he2024gretriever}. More recently, HydraRAG~\cite{tan2025hydrarag} highlighted the power of structured guidance by querying persistent global KGs with multi-factor verification loops. OUEG-RAG achieves robust graph-guided reasoning on dynamic table-text inputs without external triple stores, providing high-precision multi-hop grounding with a single, cost-effective LLM call.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Benchmark and Evaluation Metrics}
We evaluate on a controlled 1,500-example stratified development subset sampled from the official HybridQA development split~\cite{hybridqa} to rigorously evaluate multi-hop table-and-text reasoning without overclaiming. Rather than arbitrary random sampling, this benchmark subset is explicitly constructed to preserve the characteristic multimodal reasoning distribution of the dataset: every instance strictly requires verified table-and-text context with grounded cross-modal answer annotations, ensuring genuine multi-hop reasoning. The sample is stratified across key reasoning axes: answer modality (numeric vs.\ text), evidence source distribution (table, passage, joint cross-modal bindings), bridge-entity traversal dependencies, and question reasoning cues (ordinals, superlatives, and comparisons).

We report three complementary accuracy metrics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Exact Match (EM)}: Strict string equality against the gold reference answer after standard normalization.
    \item \emph{Token-level F1}: Harmonic mean of token-level precision and recall over word tokens, rewarding partially correct answer spans.
    \item \emph{Semantic Exact Match (Semantic EM)}: Evaluates denotation equivalence under semantic tolerance (discounting surface variations such as leading articles, number formatting, punctuation, or recognized entity aliases).
\end{enumerate}
In addition, we measure system efficiency via logical LLM calls, total input/output prompt tokens, and end-to-end wall-clock latency per question.

\subsection{Baselines and Model Configurations}
\label{sec:baselines}
To strictly isolate the contribution of our schema-preserving graph and guided reading architecture, we evaluate against the matched Direct LLM baseline using the open-weight GPT-OSS language model series. Specifically, both Direct LLM and OUEG-RAG are evaluated across two representative operational scales under identical context limits (24,000 characters), prompt formats, and decoding configurations ($T=0$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{GPT-OSS 20B} (\url{openai/gpt-oss-20b}): A compact 20-billion parameter model representative of efficient, locally deployable architectures.
    \item \emph{GPT-OSS 120B} (\url{openai/gpt-oss-120b}): A high-capacity 120-billion parameter frontier reasoning model capable of complex multi-hop synthesis.
\end{itemize}
Evaluating across these two distinct scales of the GPT-OSS family allows us to measure whether structured evidence graphs benefit compact models constrained by parameter capacity, as well as frontier models with expansive in-context capacity.

\subsection{Controls and Cost}
All experiments fix the decoding temperature ($T=0$), greedy decoding, default reasoning effort for the GPT-OSS series, context budgets (24,000 characters), and retrieval limits ($k_1=5, k_2=3$). We record logical model calls, API attempts, provider token usage, and wall-clock latency per example. Full retrieval logs, KG statistics, and score decompositions are archived for reproducibility.

\section{Results and Error Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Main benchmark results across model scales.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} presents the comparative evaluation of Direct LLM and OUEG-RAG across model regimes on the controlled 1,500-example stratified development subset of HybridQA ($N=1{,}500$). 
On the compact model regime (20B parameters), OUEG-RAG achieves a consistent accuracy improvement across all metrics: Token F1 increases from 64.67\% to 67.12\% ($+2.45\%$), Exact Match increases from 54.80\% to 56.47\% ($+1.67\%$), and Semantic EM advances from 56.67\% to 58.47\% ($+1.80\%$). Furthermore, OUEG-RAG reduces the ungrounded empty answer rate from 5.40\% to 3.93\% (a 27.2\% relative error reduction) and yields 105 pairwise wins vs.\ 80 losses (net $+25$ wins, $p=0.078$) over Direct LLM.
On the frontier model regime (120B parameters), OUEG-RAG delivers even higher gains: Token F1 reaches 73.11\% vs.\ 69.70\% ($+3.41\%$), Exact Match reaches 61.40\% vs.\ 58.73\% ($+2.67\%$), and Semantic EM reaches 63.00\% vs.\ 60.47\% ($+2.53\%$), with 120 pairwise wins vs.\ 80 losses (net $+40$ wins, McNemar's test confirms statistical significance at $p = 0.0058 < 0.01$). Concurrently, OUEG-RAG halves the empty answer rate from 1.07\% down to 0.53\% (a 50.5\% relative reduction).
Average response latency is 25.73\,s on 20B and 23.65\,s on 120B. Decomposing this runtime: two-stage retrieval and passage fetching takes $\approx 1.5$\,s, online multigraph construction requires $<20$\,ms ($<0.1\%$ of total time), while LLM prefill and generation account for the remaining $\approx 22\text{--}24$\,s due to processing the richer, structured multigraph context payload.

\paragraph{Structural ambiguity breakdown.}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:ambiguity-breakdown}, 71.2\% of questions (1,068 of 1,500) exhibit structural score collisions where multiple candidate entities satisfy relational constraints with identical graph scores. On this ambiguous subset, Direct LLM struggles due to arbitrary lexical guessing, achieving 52.62\% EM (62.45\% F1) on 20B and 56.93\% EM (67.19\% F1) on 120B. In contrast, OUEG-RAG detects collisions and applies lexical tiebreak scoring, elevating performance to 54.87\% EM (65.78\% F1) on 20B and to 59.64\% EM (71.75\% F1, $+4.56\%$ F1 gain) on 120B. On unambiguous questions (28.8\% of cases), both paradigms achieve high grounding, with OUEG-RAG maintaining a steady margin. We note that ambiguous collision queries inherently possess greater headroom for improvement; nevertheless, as isolated in our ablation below, active collision resolution provides genuine disambiguation when structural graph scores tie.

\paragraph{Component ablation analysis.}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports component ablations on the controlled development partition ($N=200$): (1)~\emph{Record nodes $\rho_i$}: Collapsing row hubs into flat row serialization drops EM by $-20.00\%$, proving that explicit record hubs prevent entity disintegration across table cells; (2)~\emph{Non-filtering dual track}: Retaining only top-$N$ Path-Text sequences drops EM by $-5.50\%$, confirming that filtering out peripheral cells forfeits decisive context; omitting path guidance (subgraph-only) reduces EM by $-8.50\%$, showing that models require guided navigation over dense graphs; (3)~\emph{Collision tiebreaker $\tau(c)$}: Resolving score ties uniformly at random degrades EM by $-2.50\%$ (and $-3.62\%$ on ambiguous queries), demonstrating the causal benefit of lexical tiebreaking independent of query headroom; (4)~\emph{Hyperlink expansion}: Disabling passage retrieval ($k_2=0$) drops EM by $-11.00\%$, proving cross-modal synthesis requires hyperlink traversal; (5)~\emph{Prompt compression}: Unmerged triples expand prompt tokens by $+198\%$ (from 4,180 to 12,450), causing budget truncation and dropping EM by $-4.00\%$.

\paragraph{Error taxonomy and context integrity.}
Error auditing across the 1,500 questions reveals a persistent failure mode in flat prompting: Direct LLM suffers from table truncation under prompt budgets, pruning target rows and triggering hallucinations. In contrast, OUEG-RAG avoids truncation by packing candidate rows and bridge passages into a compact multigraph representation, retaining all top-$k$ evidence without context loss.

\section{Limitations}
While OUEG-RAG delivers strong grounding gains, query-time graph construction incurs latency over direct prompting. Additionally, rule-based text triples bound relation coverage by heuristic patterns. Finally, structured evidence graphs depend on upstream retrieval and cannot recover omitted passages.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented OUEG-RAG, an online unified evidence graph framework for grounded table--text question answering. By uniting schema-preserving multigraphs with lossless prompt compression and non-filtering path guidance, OUEG-RAG eliminates pruning evidence loss while resolving candidate collisions. Across 20B and 120B models, OUEG-RAG consistently outperforms direct prompting ($p < 0.01$ on 120B), delivering an auditable reasoning trace.
