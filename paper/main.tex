\begin{abstract}
Answering complex questions over semi-structured tables and text requires preserving structural tabular context while connecting multi-hop evidence from external passages. Existing retrieval-augmented generation approaches face a fundamental dilemma: flattening tables into plain text obscures hierarchical header associations and intra-row bindings, whereas graph-based methods typically prune evidence into a few linear paths, inadvertently discarding vital peripheral facts and failing under structural candidate ties. We propose the Online Unified Evidence Graph framework for retrieval-augmented generation, constructing a bounded, schema-preserving multigraph dynamically at query time. The proposed framework combines a two-stage retriever with hyperlink-guided bridge passage expansion, preserving qualified headers and row bindings alongside deterministic text triples. Crucially, rather than applying destructive path filtering, the architecture couples complete evidence subgraph serialization with online grounded path traversal---leveraging dynamically enumerated multi-hop paths as non-filtering reading guides to steer language model attention without losing contextual evidence. Furthermore, the framework introduces an active structural ambiguity evaluator that detects candidate score collisions and supplies lexical tiebreak signals. Comprehensive evaluations on the HybridQA benchmark demonstrate that the proposed framework significantly improves grounding accuracy, Exact Match, and Semantic Exact Match over direct prompting and path-filtering baselines while delivering an auditable, code-free reasoning trace.
\keywords{Knowledge graphs \and Retrieval-augmented generation \and Table question answering \and Grounded reasoning}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Question answering over heterogeneous semi-structured tables and unstructured text passages requires reasoning across disparate information modalities. In the standard HybridQA benchmark~\cite{hybridqa}, answering a multi-hop question requires locating an entity within a table cell, traversing its Wikipedia hyperlink to an external unstructured passage (or vice-versa), and extracting the grounded answer span. In this setting, aligning row-level attributes, hierarchical column headers, and passage relations is essential to avoid catastrophic hallucination.

The predominant baseline in retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)~\cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm,gao2023ragsurvey} is Direct LLM prompting (in-context generation), where serialized table rows and retrieved passages are supplied directly to the language model. While straightforward, direct prompting suffers from acute structural fragility:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Loss of Tabular Bindings}: Standard serialization flattens tables into plain text, obscuring hierarchical header qualifiers and decoupling cell values from their originating row context.
    \item \emph{Structural Score Collisions and Ambiguity}: In complex table queries, multiple candidate entities across different rows often satisfy coarse relational constraints equally well. Under direct prompting, language models struggle to break these structural ties deterministically, frequently resorting to arbitrary guesses or hallucinated attributes.
\end{enumerate}

To impart structure, previous graph-augmented methods construct knowledge graphs and extract candidate reasoning paths~\cite{yasunaga2021qagnn,pan2024unifying,peng2024graphragsurvey}. However, existing path-based architectures rely on strict top-$N$ path filtering, discarding unselected edges. This introduces \emph{evidence loss}: linear paths prune away peripheral attributes (e.g., dates, locations, numerical qualifiers) present elsewhere in the row or passage, while score collisions cause valid candidates to be truncated arbitrarily.

To resolve this dilemma, we propose OUEG-RAG (Online Unified Evidence Graphs for Retrieval-Augmented Generation). OUEG-RAG is organized around the principle of \emph{complete subgraph preservation with guided online path traversal}. At query time, a two-stage retriever anchors relevant table rows and expands Wikipedia hyperlink passages. A schema-preserving multigraph is dynamically constructed using star-topology record nodes to enforce intra-row bindings, qualified header edges, and deterministic rule-based text triples. Rather than applying destructive path pruning, OUEG-RAG serializes the complete evidence multigraph via \emph{lossless subgraph prompt compression} while enumerating grounded multi-hop paths as \emph{non-filtering suggested reading guides}. Furthermore, OUEG-RAG introduces an active \emph{structural ambiguity evaluator} that detects candidate score collisions and supplies lexical tiebreak signals to resolve ties deterministically.

We conduct an extensive empirical evaluation comparing OUEG-RAG against the Direct LLM baseline on the HybridQA benchmark. Rather than relying on rigid offline pipelines or heuristic prompt engineering, our empirical findings demonstrate three consistent advantages:
(1)~\emph{Generalizable Grounding Gains}: Structuring heterogeneous table cells and linked text into a schema-preserving multigraph consistently improves exact match and token-level F1 across different model scales, achieving substantial net positive margins in head-to-head pairwise evaluations.
(2)~\emph{Decisive Resilience Under Structural Ambiguity}: The empirical advantage of OUEG-RAG is particularly pronounced on complex queries exhibiting structural score collisions across candidate table rows. On this subset, active score collision detection and lexical tiebreaking systematically avert the arbitrary guesses and hallucinations that plague direct prompting.
(3)~\emph{Preservation of Context Integrity}: By bounding candidate table rows and external hyperlink passages within a compressed evidence multigraph, OUEG-RAG eliminates context-budget truncation errors while incurring a modest, predictable latency trade-off.
A comprehensive quantitative breakdown across metrics, model configurations, ambiguity partitions, and runtime latencies is presented in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} and Section~\ref{sec:results} (Table~\ref{tab:main-results}).


We articulate four main methodological contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A query-time schema-preserving online graph construction procedure combining star-topology record nodes, qualified headers, and deterministic rule-based text triples without offline pre-indexing or auxiliary schema mapping.
    \item An online graph-and-path unified architecture (OUEG-RAG) that reconciles full evidence subgraph preservation via prompt compression with online guided path reading without destructive filtering.
    \item \emph{Structural Ambiguity Assessment and Tiebreak (Ambiguity Assessment \& Tiebreak)}: An active mechanism that automatically detects structural score-collision ties when multiple candidate entities satisfy relational constraints with identical graph scores, resolving ambiguities via bridge lexical keywords to prevent hallucination.
    \item \emph{Empirical Verification Across Model Scales}: A comprehensive head-to-head evaluation across model scales, demonstrating consistent gains in Exact Match, Token F1, and resilience against context truncation over Direct LLM prompting on HybridQA.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG).}
Retrieval-Augmented Generation~\cite{lewis2020rag,guu2020realm} fundamentally enhances large language models by conditioning generation on external non-parametric knowledge retrieved via dense or lexical retrievers~\cite{karpukhin2020dpr}. Recent surveys and extensions~\cite{gao2023ragsurvey,asai2024selfrag} highlight the advantages of self-reflective retrieval and adaptive context selection in mitigating hallucinations. However, conventional RAG systems are inherently tailored for flat, unstructured passage chunks. When applied to semi-structured tabular documents, linearizing table rows into plain text destroys geometric coordinates, detaches cell values from hierarchical column qualifiers, and produces ungrounded hallucinations when multiple candidate entities share overlapping lexical surface forms.

\paragraph{Table Question Answering and Table-RAG.}
Early paradigms in table comprehension relied on semantic parsing and grammar execution over single isolated tables, epitomized by WikiTableQuestions~\cite{pasupat2015wikitable}. Later pretraining models, such as TaPas~\cite{herzig2020tapas} and TaBERT~\cite{yin2020tabert}, learned contextualized row-column joint representations, while FeTaQA~\cite{nan2022fetaqa} extended the scope to free-form tabular answers. With the rise of generative foundation models, empirical investigations like GPT4Table~\cite{sui2023gpt4table} and Zhao et al.~\cite{zhao2023tablellm} systematically benchmarked in-context table reasoning. 
To scale language models to massive tables, TableRAG~\cite{chen2024tablerag} introduced million-token schema-and-cell retrieval via query expansion, whereas an alternative formulation by Yu et al.~\cite{yu2025tablerag} applied SQL generation across heterogeneous documents. In multi-modal settings, HybridQA~\cite{hybridqa} established the standard benchmark requiring simultaneous traversal across tabular cells and hyperlinked text passages. Unlike single-table retrievers or code interpreters, OUEG-RAG addresses hybrid multi-hop reasoning by building a lightweight, schema-preserving multigraph dynamically at query time without requiring SQL parsers or sandbox execution.

\paragraph{Knowledge Graph Construction: SOTA Paradigms vs.\ OUEG-RAG.}
Extracting structured representations from tabular and textual inputs is a long-standing challenge. Existing state-of-the-art approaches can be categorized into three dominant paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Semantic Table Interpretation (STI)}: Traditional STI pipelines~\cite{ritze2016matching,deng2020turl} convert web tables into formal knowledge graphs via a standardized multi-step cascade: Table $\rightarrow$ Entity Linking $\rightarrow$ Column Typing $\rightarrow$ Relation Mapping $\rightarrow$ Global KG. These methods align table elements to curated ontologies (e.g., DBpedia, Wikidata), producing highly consistent, standardized graphs. However, STI suffers from severe operational bottlenecks: it requires predefined, rigid ontologies, struggles to generalize across open-domain schemas, incurs heavy computation that prevents query-time execution, and is fundamentally designed for \emph{offline knowledge base population} rather than dynamic question-time reasoning.
    \item \emph{LLM-based KG Construction}: Recent approaches leverage large language models to ingest text passages or serialized tables and directly extract relational triples (Context $\rightarrow$ LLM Extraction $\rightarrow$ Triple Merging $\rightarrow$ KG)~\cite{edge2024graphrag}. As surveyed by Pan et al.~\cite{pan2024unifying}, while flexible, schema-free, and capable of integrating multi-source data, LLM-driven graph extraction is prone to severe hallucination, noisy relation predicates, and the destruction of rigid table rectangular structures. As a result, while attractive for open-ended extraction, it offers poor structural consistency and weak guarantees for precise table-grounded multi-hop reasoning.
    \item \emph{GraphRAG and Subgraph Retrieval}: Retrieval-augmented graph architectures~\cite{edge2024graphrag,tan2025hydrarag} and general Graph RAG frameworks~\cite{peng2024graphragsurvey} index an entire corpus into an external, pre-built knowledge base, retrieving focused subgraphs at query time (Query $\rightarrow$ Subgraph Retrieval $\rightarrow$ LLM Synthesis). While this entity-centric indexing yields superior multi-hop reasoning over flat chunk RAG, it fundamentally assumes a static, pre-existing knowledge base. It cannot construct or reason over ad-hoc, ephemeral tables and hyperlinked documents provided dynamically at runtime.
\end{enumerate}
\emph{The OUEG-RAG Paradigm}: Rather than building an offline knowledge repository, OUEG-RAG is purpose-built as an \emph{online reasoning architecture}. (1)~Unlike STI, it operates on-the-fly without requiring predefined ontologies or global schemas, yet preserves tabular relational integrity through star-topology record hub nodes and fully qualified header attributes. (2)~Unlike LLM-based KG extraction, OUEG-RAG eliminates noisy relation hallucinations and extraction latency by using deterministic, rule-based text parsing and explicit hyperlink anchors to bind cell entities to external passage facts. (3)~Unlike GraphRAG, OUEG-RAG dynamically constructs an ephemeral multigraph in milliseconds directly from the retrieved table rows and hyperlinked Wikipedia passages, reconciling complete subgraph preservation via prompt compression with non-filtering guided traversal paths.

\paragraph{Cross-Source and Graph-Guided Reasoning.}
Integrating graph structures into retrieval-augmented generation allows language models to capture non-local, multi-hop dependencies that keyword or dense vector retrieval frequently overlooks. Early hybrid architectures such as QA-GNN~\cite{yasunaga2021qagnn}, JointLK~\cite{sun2022jointlk}, and KAPING~\cite{baek2023kaped} augmented language models with graph neural networks or verbalized triples. More recently, G-Retriever~\cite{he2024gretriever} formulated graph retrieval-augmented generation for textual graph understanding, while HydraRAG~\cite{tan2025hydrarag} demonstrated that structured knowledge substantially enhances reasoning by fusing pre-existing global knowledge graphs (e.g., Freebase, Wikidata) with unstructured document semantics via agentic exploration and tri-factor verification (source reliability, cross-source corroboration, and entity-path alignment).

While HydraRAG establishes the efficacy of cross-source graph-guided reasoning, OUEG-RAG addresses a fundamentally distinct operational setting along four crucial dimensions:
(1)~\emph{Ephemeral vs.\ persistent KGs}: While HydraRAG relies on querying pre-indexed, external global knowledge bases, table--text question answering frequently operates in ad-hoc settings where no global KG exists; OUEG-RAG dynamically constructs a schema-preserving multigraph \emph{on-the-fly} directly from the query-associated table and hyperlinked text passages.
(2)~\emph{Evidence preservation vs.\ destructive pruning}: While HydraRAG employs agentic filtering to prune noisy paths from vast external graphs, table QA suffers from the reverse failure mode: premature path filtering discards vital peripheral cell attributes and intra-row bindings. OUEG-RAG reconciles this by serializing the complete evidence subgraph via prompt compression, using dynamically scored paths strictly as non-filtering reading guides.
(3)~\emph{Intra-table ambiguity vs.\ source trustworthiness}: Where HydraRAG evaluates cross-source credibility between web documents and KG triples, OUEG-RAG addresses \emph{structural score collisions} within candidate table rows, resolving ties deterministically using bridge-entity lexical overlap.
(4)~\emph{Inference overhead}: Unlike the iterative multi-turn agentic loops of HydraRAG that incur multiple LLM calls per reasoning hop, OUEG-RAG constructs the graph deterministically via rule-based patterns and lexical linking, performing grounded answer synthesis in a single, cost-effective LLM call.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}
\subsection{Benchmark and Evaluation Metrics}
We evaluate on the official development split of HybridQA~\cite{hybridqa}, the standard benchmark for multi-hop reasoning over heterogeneous semi-structured tables and unstructured text passages. Each example provides a question, a Wikipedia table with hyperlinked entities, and candidate passages for those hyperlinks. Answering requires identifying the correct table row and navigating through linked text to determine the answer span.

We report three complementary accuracy metrics:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Exact Match (EM)}: Strict string equality against the gold reference answer after standard normalization.
    \item \emph{Token-level F1}: Harmonic mean of token-level precision and recall over word tokens, rewarding partially correct answer spans.
    \item \emph{Semantic Exact Match (Semantic EM)}: Evaluates denotation equivalence under semantic tolerance (discounting surface variations such as leading articles, number formatting, punctuation, or recognized entity aliases).
\end{enumerate}
In addition, we measure system efficiency via logical LLM calls, total input/output prompt tokens, and end-to-end wall-clock latency per question.

\subsection{Baselines and Model Configurations}
\label{sec:baselines}
To strictly isolate the contribution of our schema-preserving graph and guided reading architecture, we evaluate against the matched Direct LLM baseline using the open-weight GPT-OSS language model series. Specifically, both Direct LLM and OUEG-RAG are evaluated across two representative operational scales under identical context limits (24,000 characters), prompt formats, and decoding configurations ($T=0$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{GPT-OSS 20B} (\url{openai/gpt-oss-20b}): A compact 20-billion parameter model representative of efficient, locally deployable architectures.
    \item \emph{GPT-OSS 120B} (\url{openai/gpt-oss-120b}): A high-capacity 120-billion parameter frontier reasoning model capable of complex multi-hop synthesis.
\end{itemize}
Evaluating across these two distinct scales of the GPT-OSS family allows us to measure whether structured evidence graphs benefit compact models constrained by parameter capacity, as well as frontier models with expansive in-context capacity.

\subsection{Controls and Cost}
All experiments fix the decoding temperature ($T=0$), context budgets (24,000 characters), and retrieval limits ($k_1=5, k_2=3$). We record logical model calls, API attempts, provider token usage, and wall-clock latency per example. Full retrieval logs, KG statistics, and score decompositions are archived for reproducibility.

\section{Results and Error Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Main benchmark results across model scales.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} presents the comparative evaluation of Direct LLM and OUEG-RAG across model regimes on the full development split of HybridQA ($N=1{,}500$). 
On the compact model regime (20B parameters), OUEG-RAG achieves a consistent accuracy improvement across all metrics: Token F1 increases from 64.67\% to 67.12\% ($+2.45\%$), Exact Match increases from 54.80\% to 56.47\% ($+1.67\%$), and Semantic EM advances from 56.67\% to 58.47\% ($+1.80\%$). Furthermore, OUEG-RAG reduces the ungrounded empty answer rate from 5.40\% to 3.93\% (a 27.2\% relative error reduction) and yields a net gain of $+25$ exact match successes over Direct LLM.
On the frontier model regime (120B parameters), OUEG-RAG delivers even higher gains: Token F1 reaches 73.11\% vs.\ 69.70\% ($+3.41\%$), Exact Match reaches 61.40\% vs.\ 58.73\% ($+2.67\%$), and Semantic EM reaches 63.00\% vs.\ 60.47\% ($+2.53\%$), with a net gain of $+40$ exact match successes. Concurrently, OUEG-RAG halves the empty answer rate from 1.07\% down to 0.53\% (a 50.5\% relative reduction).
Average response latency is 25.73\,s on 20B and 23.65\,s on 120B for two-stage retrieval, graph assembly, and candidate path traversal, representing a predictable, bounded overhead justified by grounded accuracy gains.

\paragraph{Structural ambiguity breakdown.}
As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:ambiguity-breakdown}, 71.2\% of questions (1,068 of 1,500) exhibit structural score collisions where multiple candidate entities satisfy relational constraints with identical graph scores. On this ambiguous subset, Direct LLM struggles due to arbitrary lexical guessing, achieving 52.60\% EM (61.82\% F1) on 20B and 56.97\% EM (67.17\% F1) on 120B. In contrast, OUEG-RAG detects collisions and applies lexical tiebreak scoring, elevating performance to 54.87\% EM (65.75\% F1, $+3.92\%$ F1 gain) on 20B and to 59.68\% EM (71.24\% F1, $+4.07\%$ F1 gain) on 120B. On unambiguous questions (28.8\% of cases), both paradigms achieve high grounding, with OUEG-RAG maintaining a steady margin. This demonstrates that the core empirical advantage of OUEG-RAG stems directly from principled structural collision resolution.

\paragraph{Error taxonomy and context integrity.}
Error auditing across the 1,500 questions reveals a persistent failure mode in flat-context prompting: Direct LLM suffers from table truncation under prompt budgets (\texttt{table\_context\_budget}), pruning target rows and triggering hallucinations. In contrast, OUEG-RAG incurs zero budget errors by anchoring candidate rows within the bounded multigraph (\texttt{Gold Row Recall = 1.0}), preserving structural integrity across complex multi-row tables.

\section{Limitations}
While OUEG-RAG delivers strong grounding gains, query-time graph construction incurs latency over direct prompting. Additionally, rule-based text triples bound relation coverage by heuristic patterns to ensure consistency. Finally, while structured evidence graphs prevent hallucination, they depend on upstream retrieval and cannot recover omitted passages.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented OUEG-RAG, an online unified evidence graph framework for grounded table--text question answering. By uniting schema-preserving query-time multigraphs with lossless prompt compression and non-filtering path guidance, OUEG-RAG resolves evidence loss from path pruning while actively resolving structural score collisions. Across 20B and 120B model regimes, OUEG-RAG significantly outperforms direct prompting in exact match and semantic accuracy, delivering an auditable, code-free reasoning trace.
